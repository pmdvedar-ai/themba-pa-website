Replace Calendar-Based APP Onboarding with Evidence-Driven Surgical Development

By Pol Martin D. Vedar, PA-C, MBA

The MBA PA

A framework for cardiac surgery programs to define readiness by case exposure — not calendar duration. Includes case complexity classification, weighted volume targets, critical case-type checklists, and structured competency gates that produce autonomous APPs faster and more consistently.

Why Calendar-Based Onboarding Produces Uneven Results — Every Single Time

Time-based onboarding doesn't fail because the timeline is wrong. It fails because time is the wrong unit of measurement for surgical competence.

What the Calendar Says
What the Cases Actually Delivered
"90 days of cardiac surgery onboarding complete"
APP shadowed 32 CABGs, 4 valves. Never managed a bleeding patient. Never cannulated independently. Never saw femoral-femoral bypass.
"Six-month competency evaluation passed"
Evaluation marked "satisfactory" by a surgeon who worked with this APP on 4 cases total. Sample too small to mean anything.
"One-year mark — independent OR assignment"
APP independent on veins for 6 months. Never harvested a radial. Never managed conduit spasm. First independent assignment pairs them with a surgeon who doesn't know their gaps.
"Standardized 12-month program for all APPs"
Three APPs hired 2 months apart: one plateaued at month 4 from boredom (came from high-volume center), one needed 2x exposure (came from low-acuity hospital), one ready at month 7. All treated identically.

The calendar doesn't measure experience. It measures attendance. And in cardiac surgery, attendance is not a clinical credential.

5) Competency Gates — Advance When Evidence Says So

Every gate has four components: Volume threshold, Surgeon attestations, Performance evidence, Gate committee decision.

Gate I: Supervised → Transitional Assist

Volume threshold: 40 weighted cases (min 25 Tier 1, 10 Tier 2, 3 Tier 3) + critical checklist complete

Surgeon attestations (min 2 attendings):

APP demonstrates safe sterile technique and OR protocol without prompting
APP can verbally anticipate next 3-5 steps in a routine CABG sequence
APP identifies and communicates abnormal findings without waiting to be asked
APP cannulation technique is consistent and safe under direct supervision

Performance evidence: 5 consecutive cannulations with ≤1 correction required • 3 consecutive chest closures with no sterile field breach • Case log review confirms diversity requirement

Gate II: Transitional → Independent Assist

Volume threshold: 80 weighted cases (min 50 Tier 1, 18 Tier 2, 6 Tier 3) + critical checklist complete

Surgeon attestations (min 3 attendings, incl. ≥1 from Tier 2/3 cases):

APP independently manages cannulation and decannulation without prompting on routine cases
APP recognizes and communicates hemodynamic changes during bypass without waiting for direction
APP independently manages chest closure including wire placement, hemostasis, and drain positioning
APP demonstrates effective communication under pressure — no freezing, no information withholding

Performance evidence: 10 consecutive cannulations with zero corrections • ≥3 cases where APP independently recognized and escalated a surgical concern before attending • ≥1 complication managed with appropriate escalation and calibration

Gate III: Independent → Full Autonomy

Volume threshold: 130 weighted cases (min 80 Tier 1, 30 Tier 2, 10 Tier 3) + all critical checklists complete

Surgeon attestations (min 4 attendings, incl. ≥2 from different surgical groups):

APP manages full operative sequence independently — anticipating surgeon needs, managing bypass transitions, directing the room during critical moments
APP independently troubleshoots: cannulation difficulty, conduit issues, unexpected findings, bleeding — escalates appropriately
APP functions as surgical team leader — directing perfusion, anesthesia communication, nursing workflow
APP has demonstrated safe independent judgment in ≥2 emergent/urgent cases

Performance evidence: 30 consecutive independent cases with zero safety events • 2 documented crisis management events with attending confirmation • All critical case types independently performed • No unresolved trust flags or trust reset protocols active

6) APP Pace Management — One Size Does Not Fit All

The Fast APP — Accelerated Advancement

An APP arrives with 3 years of cardiac surgery experience from another center.

Day 1 case audit: Map their prior log against your tier definitions and critical checklists. Where are the gaps?
Accelerated track: Compress supervised phase to what's genuinely new — not a redo of what they already know.
Gap-focused assignment: Target missing case types. If they've done 100 CABGs but never a femoral cannulation, that's priority one.
Gate eligibility: Advance when they meet your program's criteria — not when the calendar says they've "done their time."

The Slow APP — Structured Support Without Limbo

An APP at 60 weighted cases — surgeon attestations aren't coming.

Gap diagnosis: Technical? Judgment? Communication? The case log + surgeon feedback tells you.
Targeted remediation: Not "observe 10 more cases" — specific goals. "Next 5 cannulations: zero guidewire errors, zero purse-string corrections."
Remediation pairing: Assign to the surgeon who gives the most structured feedback — not the one who silently does the case and says it "didn't feel right."
Re-evaluation trigger: "After 15 additional weighted cases with documented performance, re-submit for attestation." Not "we'll check in 3 months."

The Stalled APP — When Progress Plateaus

Same competency area stalled 4+ weeks. This is usually a scheduling failure, not a performance failure.

Dashboard alert: Living case log flags any competency area with zero new cases in 30 days.
Scheduling intervention: APP lead works with OR scheduling to prioritize missing case types and surgeon pairing.
Escalation if unresolved: Gap persisting beyond 60 days despite scheduling intervention → program-level discussion.

Composite Score: ___ / 14

10–14 Competency-Driven. APPs developed by what they've done, not how long they've been employed. Surgeons trust the system because evidence is visible. APPs trust it because the path is clear.
5–9 Transitioning. Tracking cases but calendar is still primary decision tool. Advancement relies more on gut feel than structured evidence. Pieces are there — need to be connected into a system.
0–4 Calendar-Dependent. Time served, not experience earned. Fast APPs bored. Slow APPs invisible until evaluation day. Nobody knows who's actually ready for what.
